# SLR Seed Corpus

This file is the first-pass literature map for the paper on `Epistemic Type-Constraint Model`. It is not the final bibliography. It is a working corpus that separates the literature into three bands:

1. Tafsir ilmī / Qur'an-science debate
2. Hermeneutic and methodological critique
3. Formal support from philosophy of science, belief revision, and constraint-based reasoning

## Review question

How should a paper distinguish Qur'anic text, human tafsir, empirical data, and scientific models when it evaluates claims of conflict between science and the Qur'an?

## Inclusion criteria

- Directly about tafsir ilmī, Qur'an and science, or critique of scientific exegesis
- Or directly useful for the paper's formal apparatus: belief revision, constraint satisfaction, epistemic change, or data/model distinctions
- Prefer peer-reviewed journal articles and book-length academic sources
- Use theses or repository papers only when they are clearly influential or uniquely useful
- Exclude blog posts, polemics, and uncited summaries

## Seed corpus

### A. Core Qur'an-science and tafsir ilmī literature

1. **Ansari, Zafar Ishaq (2004)**. *The Scientific Exegesis (Tafsīr) of the Qur'an*. Al-Fikr Al-islāmī al-muʿāṣir, 9(35), 43-64. DOI: https://doi.org/10.35632/citj.v9i35.1449
   - Why it matters: classic critical overview of scientific exegesis; useful for framing the guidance-vs-science question and the risks of over-reading concordance.

2. **Mir, Mustansir (2004)**. *Scientific Exegesis of the Qurʾān - A Viable Project?* Journal of Islam & Science.
   - URL: https://jis.cis-ca.org/scientific-exegesis-of-the-quran-a-viable-project.html
   - Why it matters: a direct methodological challenge to the project of scientific tafsir; useful for delimiting what counts as responsible correlation.

3. **Naguib, Shuruq (2019)**. *The Hermeneutics of Miracle: Evolution, Eloquence, and the Critique of Scientific Exegesis in the Literary School of Tafsir. Part I: From Muhammad cAbduh to Amin al-Khali*. Journal of Qur'anic Studies, 21(3), 57-88. DOI: https://doi.org/10.3366/jqs.2019.0399
   - Why it matters: one of the strongest modern critiques from the literary-hermeneutic side; valuable for the paper's tafsir boundary conditions.

4. **Ismail, Ahmad Thaqif & Asnawi, Aqdi Rofiq (2021)**. *Scientific Approach in Quranic Exegesis: The Emergence and Issues*. Journal of Contemporary Islamic Studies, 7(1), 59-83.
   - URL: https://ir.uitm.edu.my/id/eprint/47134/
   - Why it matters: summarizes emergence and methodological issues; good for background and for the paper's literature review.

5. **Luqman, Muhammad Mugheerah; Nazar, Hafiz Anas; Ramzan, Tahira; Hassan, Faizan; Umair ul Haq, Muhammad; Ghumman, Ubaid ur Rehman (2023)**. *Conditions For The Scientific Interpretation Of Quran*. Journal of Positive School Psychology.
   - URL: https://journalppw.com/index.php/jpsp/article/view/15624
   - Why it matters: useful because it explicitly lays out conditions and warns against treating scientific interpretation as proof rather than reference.

6. **Qomariyah, Qomariyah & Ilyas, Ilyas (2023)**. *Pemahaman al-Dākhil dalam Tafsir Saintifik: Sebuah Tinjauan Umum*. Jurnal Semiotika-Q: Kajian Ilmu al-Quran dan Tafsir.
   - DOI: https://doi.org/10.19109/jsq.v3i2.26738
   - Why it matters: directly relevant to the paper's concern with internal intrusion or invalid interpretive moves in scientific exegesis.

7. **Mohd-Noor, Ahmad Yunus (2013)**. *Scientific Exegesis Reappraised: A Critical Study of the al-Jawāhir fī Tafsīr al-Qur'ān al-Karīm*. Doctoral thesis, Durham University.
   - URL: https://etheses.dur.ac.uk/9390/
   - Why it matters: foundational critical study of one of the major scientific exegesis works; useful for historical and methodological depth.

8. **Luckman (2024)**. *Philosophy of science in the Quran - analysing the diverse approaches on how science can be read in Quran*.
   - URL: https://qspace.qu.edu.qa/handle/10576/50524
   - Why it matters: useful for the paper's philosophy-of-science framing; especially the claim that science and the Qur'an must be handled with explicit epistemic distinctions.

9. **Bukhori Abdul Shomad; Andi Rosa; Abd. Wahid; Nasaiy Abdul Aziz; Masrizal (2022/2023)**. *Quran and Science Relations in an Epistemological Perspective: Systematic Reconstruction of 'Kawniyyah Verses' Interpretation*.
   - URL: https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=4049728
   - Why it matters: useful for comparing the paper's proposed epistemic reconstruction with an existing epistemological reconstruction of Qur'an-science relations.

10. **Ansory, Isnan & Kholis, Muhammad Nur**. *Tafsir Saintifik dalam Penafsiran Al-Qur'an: Analisis Kritis atas Epistemologi dan Metodologinya*.
    - URL: https://ejournal.iaifa.ac.id/index.php/takwiluna/article/view/2973
    - Why it matters: recent critical synthesis of epistemology and methodology; good support for the paper's boundaries and caution language.

### B. Formal apparatus for the model

11. **Alchourrón, C. E.; Gärdenfors, P.; Makinson, D. (1985)**. *On the Logic of Theory Change: Partial Meet Contraction and Revision Functions*.
   - Why it matters: the AGM baseline for belief revision; needed if the paper says revision is limited to the nazhari zone.

12. **Gärdenfors, Peter (1988)**. *Knowledge in Flux: Modeling the Dynamics of Epistemic States*.
   - Why it matters: a canonical reference for belief dynamics and epistemic state change.

13. **Chandler, Jake & Booth, Richard (2022/2023)**. *Elementary Belief Revision Operators*.
   - URL: https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10992-022-09672-6
   - Why it matters: useful if the paper wants a tighter account of revision operators than AGM alone.

14. **Thagard, Paul (1992)**. *Coherence as Constraint Satisfaction*.
   - URL: https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0364021399800330
   - Why it matters: direct support for the paper's constraint-satisfaction framing and for treating coherence as a computational problem.

15. **The Interplay of Data, Models, and Theories in Machine Learning (2025)**.
   - URL: https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/philosophy-of-science/article/interplay-of-data-models-and-theories-in-machine-learning/E6F197A54A93C3D597F1568A9EEB3B39
   - Why it matters: contemporary philosophy-of-science support for separating data, models, and theories rather than collapsing them into one level.

16. **Towards a Taxonomy of the Model-Ladenness of Data (2021)**.
   - URL: https://www.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/E00CE80C1EBF60CC80047D04073B2DF2/S0031824800016536a.pdf/towards_a_taxonomy_of_the_model-ladenness-of_data.pdf
   - Why it matters: useful for the paper's claim that data are already theory-laden and that the data/model distinction must be handled carefully.

## Working selection logic

The paper's best-supported path is not to prove that science and the Qur'an are always compatible. The stronger path is to show that many alleged conflicts are misclassified because `Q`, `Ti(Q)`, `D`, and `Mj(D)` are treated as if they belong to the same epistemic layer.

That means the final paper should lean hardest on sources that do one of three things:

- show why scientific exegesis needs constraints,
- show why interpretation is not the same as the text itself,
- or show why belief change and constraint satisfaction are legitimate formal tools for modeling limited revision.

## Notes

This corpus is intentionally mixed:
- some sources defend scientific exegesis,
- some criticize it,
- and some provide the formal machinery needed to model the paper.

That mix is deliberate. The model paper needs both critique and machinery, otherwise it turns into either apologetics or abstract formalism.
